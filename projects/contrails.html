01 · Research context & ownership

From validated mean-flow modelling to fluctuation-resolved CFD

Contrail-relevant supersaturation is governed by the coupled temperature and water-vapour field produced by turbulent exhaust mixing. The project therefore progressed in stages: validate a lower-cost mean-flow model, verify species transport, study bypass effects, construct a three-dimensional mesh, then transition to LES for time-resolved mixing statistics.

I worked under Professor Swetaprovo Chaudhuri in the UTIAS Propulsion & Energy Conversion Laboratory, with day-to-day research mentorship from Dr. Rajat Sawanni. The broader UTIAS contrail program also involves Professor Clinton P. T. Groth's Computational Fluid Dynamics & Propulsion Laboratory and Professor Ömer L. Gülder's Combustion & Propulsion Laboratory.

02 · Experimental validation

Fixing the physical model before increasing computational cost

The first planar model overpredicted downstream centreline temperature by roughly 36–43%. I tested whether inlet turbulence intensity and length-scale changes could remove the discrepancy; they did not. That pointed to dimensionality and entrainment physics rather than a parameter-tuning problem.

I replaced the planar case with a 5° axisymmetric wedge, introduced the developed core velocity profile, and used reactingFoam with chemistry disabled as a compressible non-reacting multicomponent solver. The validated case achieved 1.42% MAPE across six evaluated stations, with a 2.64% maximum error.

Engineering decision: I did not tune turbulence parameters to compensate for a geometry error. I changed the physical representation first, then used the validated axisymmetric solution as the basis for the higher-fidelity workflow.

Water-vapour transport was then checked for bounded mass fraction, mixture consistency, correct inlet origin, and sensible downstream spreading. I treated this as numerical verification rather than experimental validation, because matching concentration measurements were not available.

03 · Turbofan-relevant bypass study

Quantifying the competition between cooling and dilution

The validated axisymmetric model was extended to a core–annular-bypass configuration. I ran a nine-case matrix across BPR ≈ 2.50, 5.95, and 10.0 and bypass temperatures of 240, 270, and 300 K.

Colder bypass flow consistently produced faster cooling and earlier ice-supersaturation onset. Higher bypass ratio also moved onset upstream, while more strongly diluting core-derived water vapour. At BPR ≈ 10, reducing bypass temperature from 300 K to 240 K shifted centreline onset from 0.1600 m to 0.1123 m—nearly 48 mm upstream.

04 · Three-dimensional mesh & high-performance computing

Designing the LES around the shear-layer physics

I developed a Python-generated structured cylindrical O-grid for the three-dimensional calculation and iterated the topology using lower-cost RANS solutions. The final production mesh contained exactly 14,272,512 cells.

Rather than refine the entire tunnel uniformly, I used the developed SST-RANS turbulence field to guide where the core–ambient shear layer required the most LES resolution. This concentrated computational cost in the region responsible for the energetic turbulent structures.

05 · Solver diagnostics

Treating a numerical instability as an engineering experiment

Pressure-based LES runs developed a travelling velocity–pressure runaway when velocity shear and thermal/density shear were present together. Mesh revisions, inlet regularization, convection-scheme changes, and WALE, Smagorinsky, and dynamicKEqn closures changed the onset or growth rate but did not eliminate the failure in the tested pressure-based configurations.

Diagnostic conclusion: the controls strongly implicated pressure-based compressible coupling as an important contributor under the tested conditions. Because the density-based comparison also changed energy and mixture treatment, I did not claim solver architecture as the sole causal difference.

The stable path used rhoCentralFoam, a Kurganov central-upwind flux, van-Leer reconstruction, and dynamicKEqn. Water vapour was restored as a conservative one-way-coupled passive scalar after the stable primary-flow route was established.

06 · Late-time LES fields

Resolved turbulent structure throughout the plume

The passive-H₂O density-based LES was extended to approximately 54.5 ms after the RANS-to-LES transition. By the late state near t ≈ 0.0905 s, resolved turbulent structure had propagated throughout the plume.

07 · Supersaturation statistics

Using LES to quantify fluctuation information unavailable from deterministic RANS

I post-processed 121 snapshots from the late 48–54 ms interval. The analysis converted passive H₂O mass fraction to vapour partial pressure and then to ice-saturation ratio using the Murphy–Koop saturation-pressure relation. The primary plume mask used a time-mean water-vapour threshold of 5% of the core-inlet value, and spatial statistics used physical cell-volume weighting.

Same-mesh RANS vs LES

The RANS and LES water-vapour plume volumes differed by only about 5%, so the major difference was not bulk plume extent. LES substantially strengthened the high-Si upper tail and introduced temporal intermittency that a deterministic RANS field cannot provide.

Model scope

H₂O is transported as a one-way-coupled passive scalar and phase change is absent. The absolute Si values therefore represent thermodynamic supersaturation potential, not a microphysically equilibrated contrail. The present three-dimensional LES is the single-jet configuration; the active-bypass study remains axisymmetric URANS. Longer independent sampling windows and additional LES-resolution diagnostics are appropriate next steps.

08 · Engineering takeaways

What this project required beyond running CFD

Validate before scaling.A lower-cost model exposed a geometry/entrainment error before it could be carried into the expensive 3D LES.
Use cheaper models to design expensive ones.RANS turbulence fields helped place LES resolution where the shear-layer physics required it.
Debug with controlled experiments.Separating velocity shear, thermal/density shear, species transport, SGS model, and solver formulation made the failure interpretable.
Build an HPC workflow, not just a case.Decomposition, adaptive timesteps, restartable chunks, monitoring, reconstruction, and post-processing were integral to the research.
Turn fields into quantitative results.Plume masks, physical-volume weighting, RMS, intermittency, persistence, and percentile statistics converted snapshots into engineering conclusions.
Keep claims inside the model's limits.Thermal validation, passive-scalar assumptions, dry ambient conditions, and missing microphysics are stated separately from what the LES demonstrates.
