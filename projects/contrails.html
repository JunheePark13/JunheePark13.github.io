01 · Experimental validation

Fixing a geometry-driven 40% error before increasing fidelity

The initial planar jet model was useful for developing boundary conditions and scalar transport, but it consistently overpredicted downstream centreline temperature. Changing inlet turbulence intensity and length scale through the corresponding k and ω conditions did not remove the 36–43% error, pointing to the entrainment physics of the planar representation rather than a turbulence constant that could simply be tuned.

I replaced it with a five-degree axisymmetric wedge and progressed the solver from rhoPimpleFoam to reactingFoam with chemistry disabled for compressible air–water-vapour transport. The final case reproduced the available centreline-temperature measurements with a 1.42% mean absolute percentage error across six evaluated stations and a 2.64% maximum error.

Water-vapour transport was separately checked for bounded mass fractions, mixture consistency, and physically sensible plume spreading. It was not presented as independently validated because corresponding experimental H₂O concentration measurements were unavailable.

02 · Turbofan-relevant parameter study

Quantifying bypass cooling versus vapour dilution

The validated model was extended to the tunnel's core → nozzle wall → bypass → nozzle wall → ambient configuration rather than treating the experiment as a single isolated jet. I ran a nine-case matrix spanning bypass ratios of approximately 2.50, 5.95, and 10.0 at bypass temperatures of 240, 270, and 300 K.

The study exposed the competing physics: increasing bypass flow enhances entrainment and cools the core plume more rapidly, but also dilutes core-derived water vapour. Lower bypass temperature consistently shifted ice-supersaturation onset upstream. At BPR ≈ 10, reducing bypass temperature from 300 K to 240 K moved the first centreline location with Si > 1 from 0.1600 m to 0.1123 m — about 47.7 mm upstream.

03 · Three-dimensional RANS & mesh design

Building a purpose-designed 14.27-million-cell LES mesh

A full three-dimensional domain was required to permit azimuthal instabilities and vortex breakdown. I wrote a Python mesh generator for a structured cylindrical O-grid, using a central square to avoid the axis singularity and retaining annular regions for the core, bypass-ready geometry, ambient flow, and tunnel-wall treatment.

Instead of uniformly refining the full tunnel, I used the developed SST-RANS field to guide where LES resolution was most valuable:

Lt ≈ √k/(0.3ω)Integral turbulence length scale estimated from the RANS field.
Δ = Vcell1/3Characteristic local finite-volume cell dimension.
fres = Lt/ΔRegions with fres < 5 were targeted for additional refinement.

Successive quality checks and redesign concentrated resolution around both sides of the expanding core–ambient shear layer. The final production topology used 128 azimuthal divisions and 808 axial cells for exactly 14,272,512 cells. A developed RANS solution was then run directly on this same mesh before the LES switch, avoiding mesh-to-mesh interpolation at startup.

04 · Large-eddy simulation & high-performance computing

Resolving the unsteady mixing that RANS averages away

The LES workflow began from the developed same-mesh RANS plume and replaced the Reynolds-averaged closure with an SGS model so energetic three-dimensional turbulent motions could develop on the production mesh. The investigation included WALE, Smagorinsky, and dynamicKEqn; the current density-based route uses dynamicKEqn.

Production simulations run on SciNet's Trillium cluster with Scotch decomposition, restartable run segments, and adaptive timestepping at a maximum Courant number of 0.3. The original workflow used 192 MPI ranks, but I later tested higher parallelism. A 576-rank trial was slower, consistent with communication overhead outweighing the extra cores, so subsequent production runs use 384 MPI ranks at 128 ranks per node.

After the stable density-based flow formulation was established, water vapour was reintroduced conservatively as a one-way-coupled passive mass fraction initialized from the developed RANS H₂O field.

05 · Numerical problem solving

Turning a persistent LES failure into a controlled solver investigation

The most difficult part of the project was a travelling velocity–pressure runaway in pressure-based reactingFoam and rhoPimpleFoam LES when velocity shear and thermal/density shear were present together. I isolated the failure systematically rather than tuning one setting at a time.

Variables testedMesh topology and refinement, inlet regularization, convection schemes, timestep controls, and multiple SGS closures.
Control casesVelocity shear alone and thermal/density shear alone remained stable over their test windows, while the coupled RANS solution was also stable.
Solver resultDensity-based rhoCentralFoam with central-upwind fluxes remained bounded through more than 49 ms of LES evolution with passive H₂O transport.

The controls weakened species transport, a single defective mesh cell, boundary-condition type, and any one SGS model as complete explanations. The density-based comparison does not prove that solver architecture is the sole cause because the formulation also changes the energy and mixture treatment, but it provided the first tested 3D LES route that remained stable with the combined velocity and thermal/density gradients.

Current LES status

More than 49 ms of stable LES, with convergence checked rather than assumed

By the latest completed checkpoint, the density-based case had advanced approximately 49.1 ms beyond the RANS-to-LES switch without returning to the earlier runaway. I reconstructed saved fields and evaluated successive 3 ms windows of centreline temperature mean and standard deviation at the experimental stations to determine whether the flow was actually approaching statistical stationarity.

The late-time windows showed stable or near-stable means at several downstream stations, while some residual drift and fluctuation variation remained — particularly around the upstream 50 mm and 138.5 mm locations. Rather than call the case converged prematurely, I extended the same stable simulation again. The next step is to identify a defensible stationary sampling window and then compute resolved-fluctuation, spectral, SGS-energy, and joint temperature–water-vapour statistics for supersaturation analysis.

Engineering scope demonstrated: experimental validation, compressible CFD, turbulence modelling, species transport, parametric analysis, structured mesh generation, high-performance computing, parallel-scaling assessment, and systematic numerical debugging — applied to an industry-sponsored aerospace propulsion problem.
