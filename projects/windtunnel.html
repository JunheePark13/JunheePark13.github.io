Design & Engineering Process

This project focused on the design and fabrication of a compact desktop wind tunnel intended for flow visualization and low-speed aerodynamic experimentation. The tunnel was designed around achieving smooth, uniform flow within tight size and power constraints while remaining fully manufacturable using desktop fabrication methods.


Technical Overview

Fusion 360 Fluid Dynamics Aerodynamics 3D Printing Flow Visualization Prototype Design


The design incorporated a settling chamber, contraction section, test section, diffuser, and suction fan configuration. A hexagonal honeycomb flow straightener was implemented to reduce swirl and lateral velocity components before the contraction region. Multiple design iterations were tested to improve printability, flow quality, and structural rigidity. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

The contraction section was designed with an area ratio close to 4:1 to accelerate flow smoothly while minimizing separation and turbulence generation. The final tunnel used a 100 mm × 100 mm test section sized to balance useful air velocities with compact dimensions suitable for desktop operation. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}


CAD Development

Initial concepts and assembly layouts were developed in Fusion 360. Several iterations of the flow-straightening honeycomb geometry were explored, including failed print attempts and redesigned mesh structures that improved manufacturability and structural integrity.


Honeycomb Mesh Iterations

The honeycomb section was one of the most critical aerodynamic components in the system. Early mesh geometries experienced print failures and insufficient structural robustness, requiring redesigns to improve printability while maintaining effective flow straightening performance.

The first-generation mesh ultimately failed during fabrication due to insufficient structural support and manufacturing limitations.

A redesigned second-generation honeycomb mesh improved manufacturability and successfully integrated into the final assembly.


Prototype Fabrication

The final prototype was manufactured using additive manufacturing techniques and assembled as a modular system for testing and iteration. Special attention was given to minimizing leakage and internal recirculation, both of which significantly impact flow uniformity in compact wind tunnels.


Flow Visualization Testing

Flow visualization experiments were conducted using smoke streams and cylindrical bluff bodies placed within the test section. These experiments were used to qualitatively observe wake formation, flow separation, and vortex structures generated downstream of the objects.
