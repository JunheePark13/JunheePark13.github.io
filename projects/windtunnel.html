Desktop Wind Tunnel

Design, fabrication, and experimental testing of a compact desktop wind tunnel for low-speed aerodynamic flow visualization and educational experimentation.


Project Overview

This project involved the design, fabrication, and experimental testing of a compact desktop wind tunnel intended for aerodynamic visualization and low-speed flow studies. The objective was to create a tunnel capable of producing sufficiently uniform flow for qualitative aerodynamic experimentation while remaining manufacturable using consumer-grade fabrication tools and compact enough for desktop operation.

The tunnel architecture consisted of a settling chamber, contraction region, test section, diffuser, and suction fan configuration. Significant attention was given to minimizing turbulence intensity, reducing swirl, and improving flow uniformity through the implementation of a hexagonal honeycomb flow straightener and carefully selected contraction ratios.

The project required iterative engineering design, CAD development, manufacturing optimization, and experimental validation through smoke visualization testing.

Design Methodology

The settling chamber was designed with dimensions of approximately 180 mm × 180 mm and incorporated a honeycomb flow straightener to remove lateral velocity components and large-scale swirl structures. A contraction ratio near 4:1 was selected to accelerate the flow smoothly into the test section while minimizing separation and turbulence generation. These values were selected based on recommendations from wind tunnel design literature and compact subsonic tunnel studies.

The test section dimensions were chosen to balance useful air velocities with the flow limitations of a single 120 mm fan operating in suction mode. The suction configuration was intentionally selected over a pushing configuration due to improved downstream flow quality and reduced upstream turbulence generation.

A diffuser section was added downstream of the test section to improve pressure recovery and reduce backflow tendencies into the fan assembly. The overall tunnel length was minimized to reduce internal losses while maintaining acceptable flow conditioning performance.

CAD Development & Assembly

The complete wind tunnel assembly was modeled in Fusion 360 prior to fabrication. Multiple subsystem layouts were explored to optimize component integration, manufacturability, and internal flow geometry. Particular emphasis was placed on maintaining smooth internal transitions between the settling chamber, contraction section, and test section.

Honeycomb Mesh Development

The honeycomb flow straightener was one of the most critical aerodynamic components in the tunnel. The structure was intended to reduce swirl and lateral velocity components prior to the contraction region, thereby improving downstream flow uniformity.

Initial mesh geometries experienced fabrication failures due to insufficient structural support and print instability. Several redesigns were therefore performed to improve manufacturability while preserving aerodynamic performance and geometric consistency.

Prototype Fabrication

The final prototype was manufactured primarily through additive manufacturing techniques and assembled as a modular system to allow iterative modifications and testing. Particular attention was given to reducing leakage paths and internal recirculation regions, both of which significantly degrade flow uniformity in compact wind tunnel systems.

Flow Visualization & Experimental Testing

Smoke flow visualization experiments were conducted to qualitatively evaluate flow behavior inside the test section. Cylindrical bluff bodies were introduced into the flow to generate wake structures and visualize flow separation and vortex formation downstream of the objects.

These experiments demonstrated the tunnel’s ability to produce coherent flow structures suitable for educational aerodynamic testing and basic low-speed flow visualization studies.
