UTAT UAS · Aerodynamics

Competition aircraft design, analysis, and fabrication.

A collection of my work within the University of Toronto Aerospace Team’s UAS division. The project combined aerodynamic modelling, configuration analysis, manufacturing, CAD, and exploratory R&D.

Technical workstreams

Six connected projects within one aircraft program.

Together, these workstreams cover aerodynamic analysis, CAD and tooling, manufacturing, verification, optimization, and experimental R&D across the aircraft program.

01

R&D · Propeller–airframe interaction

OpenVSP Prop-Wash R&D

Exploratory aerodynamic analysis of how propeller slipstreams influence local wing loading and aircraft behaviour. The work used OpenVSP-based modelling to compare configurations with and without propeller effects and to assess how useful the tool could be for design decisions.

  • Configured aircraft and propeller geometry for comparative studies.
  • Examined changes in spanwise loading and integrated aerodynamic coefficients.
  • Documented modelling assumptions, limitations, and potential validation needs.

02

Manufacturing · Structural assembly

Wing Construction

Hands-on fabrication and assembly work supporting the competition-aircraft wing. This work connected the designed geometry to the practical constraints of building a lightweight balsa structure accurately and repeatably.

  • Worked with laser-cut balsa components, 3D-printed parts, jigs, and structural assembly.
  • Used alignment tooling to maintain geometry during construction.
  • Considered tolerances, joining methods, manufacturability, repairability, and build sequence.
ManufacturingLaser cuttingBalsa3D printingAssembly
03

CAD · Tooling · Design for manufacture

CAD & Wing Tooling

CAD work focused on adapting manufacturing fixtures and wing-support tooling to updated aircraft geometry. The task was not only to make parts fit digitally, but to preserve the clearances, alignment, and accessibility needed for the physical wing build.

  • Updated jig and post geometry to match revised wing outer-mold-line and component locations.
  • Adjusted fixture positions and clearances while maintaining alignment with the wing assembly.
  • Worked through assembly mates, component edits, cuts, duplicated tooling features, and tolerance changes in SolidWorks.
  • Used the CAD model as a manufacturing interface between evolving aircraft geometry and repeatable shop-floor assembly.

04

Verification · Aerodynamic tools

Flow5 vs. OpenVSP

A comparison of aerodynamic predictions from Flow5 and OpenVSP/VSPAERO for representative aircraft configurations. The study focused on understanding agreement, disagreement, and the modelling choices responsible for each result.

  • Compared lift, induced drag, and spanwise loading trends.
  • Investigated geometry, discretization, and solver-setting differences.
  • Used an elliptical loading reference where appropriate to interpret results.

Optimization · Configuration analysis

MDO & Constraint Analysis

Development of the fuselage contribution within a multidisciplinary aircraft-design workflow. My role involved configuring models for fuselage drag, aerodynamic contribution, and weight so the optimizer could evaluate complete aircraft configurations rather than the wing alone.

  • Integrated fuselage skin-friction and form-factor drag estimates into the drag build-up.
  • Investigated approaches for representing fuselage lift and aerodynamic interactions.
  • Developed or reviewed fuselage-weight estimation for use in payload and constraint calculations.
  • Supported comparisons between optimized geometry and external aerodynamic tools.
MATLABMDODrag build-upWeight estimation
06

Experimental R&D · Additive manufacturing

Fully 3D-Printed Wings

Design and experimentation with fully 3D-printed wing structures as an alternative manufacturing route. The work explored geometry, internal structure, print orientation, assembly, stiffness, surface quality, and mass.

  • Iterated internal mesh and structural concepts for printable wing sections.
  • Assessed print failures, geometric fidelity, and assembly strategies.
  • Compared manufacturing simplicity against structural and aerodynamic compromises.
