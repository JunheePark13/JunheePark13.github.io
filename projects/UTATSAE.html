UTAT Aerodynamics

Aerodynamic analysis, design support, manufacturing, and flight-test experience for the University of Toronto Aerospace Team SAE aircraft.


Project Overview

I worked on the aerodynamics subteam for UTAT SAE, contributing to the design, analysis, manufacturing, and testing of a VTOL aircraft for the SAE Aero Design Advanced Class competition. The aircraft achieved a podium finish at the international competition.

My work focused on using aerodynamic analysis tools to support design decisions, especially OpenVSP/VSPAero and XFLR5. I investigated wing and propeller interactions, prop wash effects, preliminary wing configurations, airfoil selection, and the practicality of using OpenVSP as an internal design and analysis tool.

OpenVSP & VSPAero Development

A major part of my contribution involved researching OpenVSP as an aerodynamic modeling and analysis tool. I used OpenVSP to model aircraft geometry, including wings and propellers, and explored how VSPAero could be used to estimate aerodynamic behavior and prop wash effects.

This required troubleshooting geometry setup, wake modeling, propeller disk assumptions, paneling choices, convergence settings, and interpretation of spanwise aerodynamic outputs. I also cross-checked results with XFLR5 to understand whether outputs were physically reasonable.

Troubleshooting & Validation

OpenVSP/VSPAero results required careful interpretation. Some early outputs were clearly nonphysical or inconsistent, which led to troubleshooting of geometry setup, paneling, propeller modeling assumptions, and solver settings.

Rather than treating simulation output as automatically correct, I used unexpected results as a debugging signal. This helped develop a more critical workflow for aerodynamic analysis: compare against intuition, check geometry setup, cross-reference with simpler tools, and identify when a result is likely numerical or setup-related.

Preliminary Wing Design

I helped with preliminary aerodynamic design decisions, including wing configuration, airfoil considerations, box-wing design discussions, and early tradeoffs between lift, stability, manufacturability, and integration with VTOL propulsion.

I am also currently reviewing the team’s Python-based multidisciplinary design optimization workflow for preliminary design, with the goal of better understanding how geometry, aerodynamic performance, and mission-level constraints are connected.

Manufacturing & Assembly

Beyond analysis, I contributed to hands-on manufacturing and assembly. This included 3D printing components, preparing laser-cut diagrams, using jigs and fabrication tools, gluing structural components, helping assemble the wings, and applying Monokote covering.

This was valuable because it connected aerodynamic design decisions to real manufacturing constraints. Design choices had to be not only aerodynamically reasonable, but also buildable, alignable, repairable, and compatible with team manufacturing timelines.

Aircraft & Competition

The final aircraft integrated aerodynamic design, structural manufacturing, propulsion, controls, and flight testing into a competition-ready system. Working on this project gave me experience with the full engineering cycle: analysis, design, fabrication, testing, troubleshooting, and iteration.

Flight Testing

Flight testing provided a direct connection between design assumptions and aircraft behavior. The first flight test included runway takeoff and landing, giving the team a chance to observe aircraft performance, stability, and integration issues in a real operating environment.

First flight test showing runway takeoff and landing.

My Contributions

My main contributions included R&D on OpenVSP/VSPAero as an aerodynamic tool, modeling wings and propellers, studying prop wash interactions, validating and cross-checking results using XFLR5, assisting with preliminary design decisions, and contributing to the manufacturing and assembly of the aircraft.

I also worked with SolidWorks for component-level CAD tasks and supported physical fabrication through 3D printing, laser-cut preparation, gluing, assembly, and Monokote covering. I am currently reviewing the team’s Python-based MDO workflow for preliminary aircraft design.

Key Takeaways

This project taught me that aerodynamic analysis is only useful when paired with careful validation and manufacturing awareness. Tools like OpenVSP and VSPAero can be powerful, but their results depend heavily on modeling assumptions, geometry setup, and interpretation.

The project also strengthened my understanding of aircraft design as an integrated process involving aerodynamics, structures, propulsion, CAD, manufacturing, and testing rather than isolated calculations.
