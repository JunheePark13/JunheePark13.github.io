Project Overview

This project compared Euler’s Method, Improved Euler’s Method, fourth-order Runge-Kutta (RK4), and a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) for solving nonlinear ordinary differential equations. The Brusselator model was selected as the benchmark system due to its oscillatory and nonlinear behavior.

The study evaluated the methods in terms of accuracy, computational cost, stability, and qualitative solution behavior across different dynamical regimes.

Mathematical Model

The Brusselator is a nonlinear autocatalytic reaction model governed by two coupled ordinary differential equations:

dx/dt = A − (B + 1)x + x²y


dy/dt = Bx − x²y

Depending on the selected parameter values, the system can exhibit stable steady states, oscillations, and limit-cycle behavior, making it useful for evaluating both classical numerical solvers and neural-network-based approximations.

Methods Compared

Euler’s Method Improved Euler RK4 Physics-Informed Neural Networks

Euler’s Method served as the simplest first-order explicit baseline. Improved Euler introduced a predictor-corrector approach with improved local accuracy, while RK4 provided a higher-order numerical reference solution with significantly stronger convergence behavior.

The PINN was trained using a combined loss function incorporating differential equation residuals, initial conditions, and supervised solution data generated from RK4 reference trajectories.

Key Findings

RK4 consistently achieved the best accuracy-performance tradeoff, producing stable and highly accurate solutions even at larger time steps. Improved Euler provided moderate improvements over Euler’s Method while maintaining relatively low computational cost.

The PINN successfully captured some global solution behavior, but its performance was highly regime-dependent. In highly oscillatory or stiff parameter regions, the network experienced phase inaccuracies and reduced generalization quality.

One of the primary conclusions of the study was that physics-informed neural networks are not automatically superior to classical numerical methods. For low-dimensional nonlinear ODE systems such as the Brusselator, traditional time-stepping methods remained more reliable and computationally efficient.
